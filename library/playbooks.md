# Trade playbooks

Starting points for the customer brief. The form's own answers always win over these. Use a playbook for
thinking, never as a layout to copy. If the trade isn't here, use the closest one.

## Bakery, cafe, food counter
- Visitor: a neighbor on a phone, often already walking. Question: "Are you open, and is there still some left?"
- First screen: live open/closed status in plain words, what's on the counter today with prices, the address with a map link.
- Often useful: pre-order steps, a short menu that reads like a counter board, holiday notice.
- Avoid: "Our story" first, script logos, a long origin essay, 24-hour times.

## Restaurant or bar
- Visitor: deciding tonight, on a phone. Questions: "Can I get a table? What's on the menu? What does it cost?"
- First screen: booking button (OpenTable, Resy, phone), today's hours, the neighborhood.
- Often useful: the real menu with prices as text (not a PDF), a few dishes, private events if offered.
- Avoid: autoplay video, menus as images, a hero slideshow.

## Health clinic (physical therapy, chiropractic, dental, therapy)
- Visitor: in pain or worried, on a phone. Questions: "Do I need a referral? Do you take my insurance? Who will I see? How soon?"
- First screen: answers to those questions in plain words, the booking action, today's hours.
- Often useful: what happens at the first visit, the clinician with credentials and a real photo, insurance list.
- Avoid: stock smiling-therapist photos, teal-and-white chain look, medical claims the form doesn't make.

## Home trades (plumber, electrician, roofer, cleaner, landscaper)
- Visitor: something is broken or overdue, often on a phone. Questions: "Do you cover my area? How fast? What will it cost? Are you licensed?"
- First screen: call or text button, service area, response time if given, license/insurance if given.
- Often useful: price ranges or "from" prices, before/after photos with real captions, the steps of a job.
- Avoid: generic tool icons, "quality service" slogans, invented guarantees.

## Mobile or appointment service (groomer, mobile mechanic, tutor, photographer on location)
- Visitor: booking from a phone. Questions: "Do you come to me? How much? When can you come?"
- First screen: availability in words ("Booking this week", "Back on the road Tuesday"), the towns covered, prices.
- Often useful: a sticky Book / Text bar on phones, what a visit looks like, a request form with the details the business needs.
- Avoid: paw-print or cartoon decoration, maps that are decoration rather than an answer.

## Salon, barber, spa
- Visitor: ready to book, on a phone. Questions: "Who will do my hair? What does it cost? When is there an opening?"
- First screen: book button (Square, Vagaro, Fresha), the stylists by name, price list.
- Often useful: real work photos with the stylist's name, cancellation policy, parking.
- Avoid: gradient "luxury" looks, stock models, prices hidden behind the booking widget.

## Professional services (bookkeeper, accountant, lawyer, consultant)
- Visitor: at a desk, comparing two or three options. Questions: "What will it cost? Who will do the work? Can you handle my situation?"
- First screen: what they do in plain words, how pricing works (a real "from" price if possible), the booking action.
- Often useful: the named person with credentials and a photo, the first three steps of working together, who they're for.
- Avoid: handshake photos, "trusted partner" copy, a wall of service cards.

## Creative studio (architect, interior designer, photographer, florist)
- Visitor: comparing studios at a desk, unhurried. Question: "Is their work what I want, and would they take my project?"
- First screen: the work, large, with very little text. A short line on what they take on.
- Often useful: projects as short stories (place, brief, result), who the people are, how to start (what to send in a first email).
- Avoid: thumbnail grids of everything, philosophy paragraphs, a slideshow homepage.

## Gym, studio, class-based business
- Visitor: deciding whether to try it. Questions: "What's the schedule? What does it cost? Is it for beginners?"
- First screen: the next class (library helper), the intro offer if given, the booking action.
- Needs from the client: the full class schedule (class, day, time). Ask for it if only opening hours are given.
- Often useful: what a first class is like, coaches by name, membership prices.
- Avoid: aggressive all-caps hype, stock fitness photos, fake "transformation" stats.

## Florist, gift and delivery shop
- Visitor: needs something today, on a phone. Questions: "Can you deliver today? What does it cost? Can I pick up?"
- First screen: the same-day cutoff and the days it applies, starting prices, the order button.
- Often useful: sympathy and event sections kept separate from everyday orders, delivery area, pickup details.
- Avoid: photos that imply the wrong occasion (red roses beside sympathy), generic stock bouquets.

## Shop or retailer
- Visitor: local, checking before a visit. Questions: "Do you have what I need? When are you open? Where do I park?"
- First screen: what they sell in one line, open/closed status, address and parking.
- Often useful: a few categories or brands carried, events, a link to online orders if they exist.
- Avoid: building an online store; link to an existing one instead.
