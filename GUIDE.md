# Start your website business

A plain guide to turning this kit into a small business: who to approach, what to say, what to charge, and how to deliver.

---

## What you're selling

> "I make clean, modern websites for local businesses. You get a site that works on phones, shows your hours, prices and how to reach you, and I keep it updated for a small monthly fee."

One package keeps it simple:
- **A new website**, one-time price.
- **Monthly care**: updates (hours, prices, holiday notices, photos) and keeping the site online.
- **The client pays for their own domain name** and owns it.

Use the `pricing` page in this kit to set your numbers.

---

## Before your first client (about a week)

1. **Set up the kit** (see the README).
2. **Make two sample sites.** Use a friend's or family member's business, or a business you like (as a practice piece, not published). Samples are what win clients: being able to show one on your phone matters more than anything you say.
3. **Set your prices** with the `pricing` page.
4. **Set up a way to get paid.** A free Square, Stripe or PayPal account lets you send invoices clients pay by card, including automatic monthly billing. The kit's `invoice` page covers anything else.
5. **Business basics.** Many people start as a sole proprietor under their own name. Rules differ by city and state, so check your local requirements for a business license or registered name. Keep a separate bank account if you can, save every invoice and receipt, and ask an accountant how to handle taxes on this income. (This guide isn't legal or tax advice.)
6. **Copy the tracker** (`tracker/tracker.xlsx`) into Google Sheets. Every client and payment goes there.

---

## Who to approach first

The best first clients are **local, owner-run businesses** where the owner makes the decision on the spot. Trades, food, beauty, fitness, shops and services are all good.

Look for any of these:
- **No website**, only a Facebook or Instagram page.
- **A website that looks old or breaks on a phone.** Open it on your phone and see.
- **Wrong or missing basics**: no hours, no prices, no way to book.
- **Businesses you already know**: your barber, your mechanic, the café you go to.

Start with people who know you. Your first three clients will most likely come from people you already know or businesses you already use.

---

## What to say

Keep it short, friendly and about them. Have a sample site open on your phone.

- **In person:** "Hi, I'm [name]. I make websites for local businesses. I noticed [your site doesn't show your hours on a phone / you're only on Instagram]. Here's one I made for [business]. Would it help to have something like this? I can show you a draft of yours before you decide."
- **Ready-to-use messages** for email, text and Instagram are in [`templates/messages.md`](templates/messages.md).

**A draft before they pay** is a strong closer: Claude can make a first version in an hour or so. Offer it to businesses that are clearly interested, not to everyone.

**Common answers:**
- *"My nephew can do it."* "Great. If that falls through, I'm happy to help. Here's my number."
- *"I have Facebook."* "Facebook is good for regulars. A website is what new customers find on Google, with your hours, prices and how to book in one place."
- *"It's too expensive."* "The monthly care plan includes updates, so you never have to touch it. What would one extra customer a month be worth to you?"
- *"Can I change it myself?"* "You don't need to. Text me what changed and I'll update it, usually the same week."

---

## The first meeting (15 minutes)

1. Ask: "When someone finds you online, what do you want them to do?" (Call, book, walk in, order.)
2. Ask what customers usually ask before they buy. This shapes the whole site.
3. Explain the package and price, and how long it takes (usually a few days once you have their answers and photos).
4. Send them your **question page** link and ask for their **logo and 4 to 10 photos**.
5. Send the agreement ([`templates/agreement.md`](templates/agreement.md)) and an invoice for the deposit.

**Take a 50% deposit before you start.** It protects your time and shows the client is serious.

---

## Building and delivering

1. Paste their answers into a new chat in your Websites project and attach their logo and photos.
2. Send them the preview link. Collect their changes and paste them into the chat.
3. When they're happy, say "approved" and put the site online.
4. **Help them buy their domain in their own name**, and connect it to the site.
5. Send the final invoice. Set up their monthly care plan as an automatic monthly invoice.
6. Add everything to your tracker, and save their folder (answers, logo, photos, final page).

---

## After launch

- **Ask for a referral and a review** right away, while they're happy: "If you know another business owner who needs a site, I'd be grateful for an introduction."
- **Check in every few months.** Holidays, new prices and new photos are easy updates, and they remind clients why the care plan is worth it.
- **Watch the tracker** for domains renewing soon, and remind clients a few weeks ahead.

---

## Your first month

| Week | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Set up the kit, make two sample sites, set your prices, open a payment account. |
| 2 | Make a list of 20 local businesses. Contact the 10 you know best (in person or by message). |
| 3 | Follow up once with everyone who didn't answer. Hold first meetings. Take deposits. |
| 4 | Build and launch your first sites. Ask each client for a referral. Contact the next 10. |

Most people won't say yes the first time, and that's normal. Follow up once, politely, then move on.
